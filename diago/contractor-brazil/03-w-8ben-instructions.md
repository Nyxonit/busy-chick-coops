# Form W-8BEN — instructions for the contractor in Brazil

Do not sign a homemade copy of the tax form. Download the current official form and its instructions:

https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-w-8-ben

Print or fill the IRS PDF, sign it, and send the signed PDF to hello@diagoai.com. Diago keeps it. Diago does not send it to the IRS with the contractor’s first invoice. The form certifies foreign status to the payer.

## What Diago needs before the first payment

1. The signed Independent Contractor Agreement.
2. The signed Statement of Work.
3. This Form W-8BEN, signed and dated.
4. The payment-details sheet.
5. An invoice for the specific amount being paid.

## How to complete Form W-8BEN

The United States and Brazil do not have an income tax treaty. Brazil does not appear on the IRS income-tax-treaty list. Leave the treaty section blank.

**Part I — Identification of Beneficial Owner**

| Line | What to enter |
|---|---|
| 1 Name | The contractor’s legal name, matching the CPF and the agreement. |
| 2 Country of citizenship | Brazil |
| 3 Permanent residence address | A street address in Brazil. Not a PO box. Not a US address. |
| 4 Mailing address | Blank if it is the same as line 3. |
| 5 US taxpayer identification number | Blank, unless the contractor already has an SSN or ITIN. |
| 6a Foreign tax identifying number | The contractor’s CPF. |
| 6b | Check the box only if the form’s own instruction says a foreign TIN is not required. A CPF is a foreign TIN, so line 6a should be filled. |
| 7 Reference number | Optional. The contractor may write “Diago Technologies LLC”. |
| 8 Date of birth | The contractor’s date of birth, in the format the form asks for. |

**Part II — Claim of Tax Treaty Benefits**

Leave Part II blank. Do not write “Brazil,” do not invent an article number, and do not write a reduced withholding rate. There is no treaty to claim.

**Part III — Certification**

Sign, date, and print the name. The person who signs must be the beneficial owner named on line 1. A typed name without a signature is not enough if the IRS form requires a signature.

## What not to file instead

- **Form W-9** is for US persons. Do not send a W-9.
- **Form W-8BEN-E** is for entities. Use it only if a Brazilian company, not the individual, is the contracting party. This package assumes an individual.
- **Form 8233** is how an individual claims a treaty exemption on compensation for personal services. Do not send it to claim a Brazil treaty benefit. There is no US–Brazil income tax treaty.

## When to send a new form

Send a new W-8BEN if the name, address, citizenship, or CPF changes, and in any case before the signed form expires under the IRS instructions printed on it (generally the third calendar year after the year of signature, unless the facts change sooner).

## A note for Diago, not for the form

Compensation for services performed entirely outside the United States is generally foreign-source. That is not the same as US-source passive income (dividends, interest, royalties), which can be withheld at 30% when there is no treaty. Confirm sourcing, withholding, and any Form 1042 / 1042-S duty with a CPA before the first payment. Do not decide the withholding rate from this sheet alone.
